## M.Sc. Thesis — Sharif University of Technology (2023 – 2026)

**The Art of Heat Transfer: Investigating Paint Rheology and Patterns in Heat Exchangers**

<p class="thesis-abstract">
My master's thesis tackles a deceptively simple question: what happens to a heat exchanger
when you spray paint on it? The answer connects condensation physics, surface science, and fluid
rheology. I designed hybrid hydrophilic–phobic surface coatings — their tree-like branching
patterns following Adrian Bejan's Constructal Law, mimicking the water-collection geometry of
desert beetles — to shift condensation from filmwise to dropwise and enhance heat transfer
coefficients by up to an order of magnitude.
</p>

<p class="thesis-abstract">
The experimental campaign required engineering the paint itself: conventional pigments failed
(caking, chalking, staining), so I formulated reversible mineral-pigment paints with
ethanol–glycerol solvents tuned to behave as thin Newtonian fluids. Sprayed onto a fixed-geometry
plate-fin heat exchanger, coverage was quantified per-plate through a computer-vision pipeline
(color detection and DBSCAN clustering). The work spans coating design, fluid formulation,
experimental setup design, and data analysis.
</p>

## B.Sc. Thesis — Isfahan University of Technology (2023)

**Air–Water Generator Using Thermoelectric Coolers: Semi-Analytical Modeling**

<p class="thesis-abstract">
My bachelor's thesis modeled heat exchangers integrated with thermoelectric coolers (TECs) for
atmospheric water harvesting — a device that extracts drinking water from air. I developed
semi-analytical models of different heat-exchanger/TEC configurations to analyze the efficiency
of the technology, balancing thermoelectric performance against heat-exchanger design. The thesis
resulted in a journal paper on the thermo-economics of thermoelectric-based atmospheric water
harvesters.
</p>
